Health risk monitoring system 
-Measurements: 
-Physical measurements: 
Heart rate(BPM):  
Explanation: Beats per minute are measured by a wearable ECG or optical 
pulse sensor. 
Microgravity shifts fluids towards the chest and head, changing 
cardiovascular workload and altering the heart rate responses. 
Normal range: 60-100bpm 
Abnormal range: below 60 or above 100 (not always abnormal) 
Blood oxygen(%): 
Explanation: Measured by a pulse oximeter. Or can be directly determined by 
a respiratory wearable sensor. 
Oxygen saturation reflects hemoglobin oxygenation which can reveal 
circulatory problems. 
Normal range: 95-100% 
Abnormal range: Below 95%, ≤ 90% is particularly concerning 
Bone density(g/𝒄𝒎𝟐): 
Explanation: Without gravitational loading, bones remodel towards 
mineralized tissue, raising concerns about bone strength and kidney stones. 
Depends on age, sex, measured at the lumbar spine. 
Normal range: 0.8-1.2 
Abnormal range: not in the normal range, and A T-score ≤-2.5 indicates 
conditions for astronauts above the age of 50 
 
muscle strength(N): 
Explanation: Muscles experience less mechanical loading in microgravity, 
causing reduced force production, especially in muscles used for posture. 
(hand grip strength) 
Normal:290-480 (not gender specific(average))  
Abnormal: less than 290-480 (not gender specific); for women, low strength is 
around 180 N, and for men it's below 270N (gender specific values) 
Body temperature(degrees Celsius): 
Explanation: Body temperature reflects metabolic heat production and heat 
transfer, and in microgravity, convection and sweat evaporation behave 
differently, making thermal regulation important. 
Normal: 36.1-37.2 
Abnormal: 38 or higher is generally a fever 
 
- environmental conditions: 
Radiation exposure(mSv): 
Explanation: measured by a dosimeter 
. Space radiation includes energetic particles that ionize tissue and can 
damage DNA. Exposures differ with solar activity, shielding, and spacecraft 
location. 
Normal: 0.0066 daily 
Abnormal: more than 0.0066 daily 
 
- psychological health: 
Stress and mind state: 
Normal: Serene and calm 
Abnormal: Moderate stress/high tension 
Sleep(hours): 
Normal:7-8  
Abnormal: less than 7 hours, More than 10 hours may warrant attention if 
persistent or occupied by fatigue. 
Connection: 
Abnormal: lonely/very lonely 
Energy: 
Abnormal: Drained

Abnormality %=

𝑡𝑜𝑡𝑎𝑙 𝑟𝑒𝑎𝑑𝑖𝑛𝑔𝑠(𝑖𝑛 𝑎 𝑤𝑒𝑒𝑘) ×100 
 
At what abnormality % is it concerning? 
Blood oxygen & body temperature are concerning if the abnormality 
percentage is equal to or higher than 20%. 
Heart rate, bone density, muscle strength, high stress, insufficient sleep, 
loneliness, and drained energy are concerning if the abnormality percentage 
is equal to or higher than 50%. 
 
* Bone density and radiation also need longer-term (monthly) assessment 
rather than weekly abnormality counting. 
 
-Alert messages (possible conditions): 
*Only give an alert message if the alert threshold is reached. 
 
Heart rate: persistent heart rate abnormalities detected. 
Possible Cardiac arrhythmia: look for persistent unusually fast, slow, or 
irregular heartbeat. impaired with fatigue or dizziness. 
Possible stroke: look for sudden facial dropping, 1 sided weakness, speech or 
vision difficulties. 
* Emergency symptoms check for strokes: (B.E. F.A.S.T.): 
B-balance: sudden loss of balance. 
E- eyes: Sudden vision changes. 
F-face: one side of the face droops. 
A-arms: weakness in one arm. 
S-speech: slurred speech or difficulty speaking. 
T-time: immediately activate the emergency medical protocol: 
what's the crewmates should do: 
1. alert Mission Control immediately. 
2. Record the time symptoms began. 
3. Keep the astronauts safe and supported. 
4. Follow the spacecraft's emergency medical protocol. 
5. Do not give food, drink, or medication unless directed by medical 
personnel. 
Blood oxygen: repeated low blood oxygen detected. 
Possible respiratory or oxygenation impairment. 
Bone density: progressive bone density decline detected.  
Possible bone demineralization: Increased risk of bone weakness and 
fractures. 
Muscle strength: resistant muscle strength reduction detected.  
Possible muscle deconditioning. 
Body temperature: repeated temperature abnormalities detected. Possible 
infection, inflammation, or temperature regulation disturbance. 
Stress: persistent high stress detected. Possible psychological strain or 
adaptation difficulties: Resistant psychological strain and difficulty adapting 
to isolation. 
Sleep: repeated insufficient sleep detected. Possible sleep disruption and 
fatigue. 
Social connection: persistent loneliness detected. Possible social isolation 
and psychological strain. 
Energy: persistent low energy detected. Possible fatigue, inadequate 
recovery, or psychological strain 
 
* A note that can be added in each message: these thresholds should 
generate possible health concerns; not diagnosis. 
 
-Combinations of abnormal measurements(when some 
of measurements have high abnormality frequency): 
1. High heart rate+ low blood oxygen=  
Possible pulmonary embolism(blood clots in the lungs): sudden 
breathlessness, chest pain, fainting. 
Possible Hypoxia: reduced oxygenation, breathlessness, confusion. 
2. low bone density+ low muscle strength=possible musculoskeletal 
deterioration 
3. High heart rate+ high body temperature= possible heat stress, 
dehydration, infection 
4. low blood oxygen+ high heart rates+ abnormal temperature= 
possible respiratory illness or systemic infection 
5. Heart rate+ high tension stress= possible psychological stress. 
6. Reduced muscle strength+ drained energy= possible muscle atrophy: 
progressive loss of muscle performance. 
7. Abnormal heart rates+ reduced muscle strength= 
 possible cardiovascular deconditioning: reduced exercise tolerance 
and physical performance 
 
-Abnormality intervention steps: 
Intervention types: 
1- early intervention: when abnormal readings are appearing but not 
reaching the alert threshold. Give the astronauts simple actions to improve 
their condition. 
2- health alert: the abnormality has reached the alert threshold. Give more 
structured steps involving the astronaut and crewmates, or medical support. 
 
Measurements and their interventions: 
Heart rate:  
Early intervention: 1. Reduce physical activity temporarily. 2. Allow time for 
rest and recovery. 3. Maintain adequate hydration.  
Health alert: 1. Assess for symptoms such as dizziness, chest pain, or 
breathlessness. 2. Review recent activity, hydration, and sleep. 3. Perform an 
ECG if directed by the medical team. 5. Follow the prescribed cardiovascular 
assessment protocol. 
 
Blood oxygen: 
Early intervention: 1. Sit comfortably and rest. 2. Check that the monitoring 
device is positioned correctly. 3. Ensure that the cabin oxygen system is 
functioning normally. 
Health alert: 1. Assess breathing difficulties, chest discomfort, and dizziness. 
2. Evaluate cabin oxygen and pressure conditions. 3. Follow this spacecraft's 
respiratory emergency protocol. 4. Supplemental oxygen or other treatment 
should only be provided according to medical and mission protocols. 
 
Bone density: 
early intervention: 1. Maintain the prescribed resistance and weight bearing 
exercise program. 2. Follow the approved calcium and vitamin D nutrition 
plan. 3. Maintain nutritional intake.  
Health alert: 1. Assess exercise intensity and adherence. 2. Review nutritional 
intake and possible deficiencies. 3. Medical specialists may be needed to 
consider additional future investigations or treatments. 
 
Muscle strength:  
early intervention: 1. Follow the scheduled resistance exercise program. 2. 
Allow sufficient recovery between sessions. 3. Maintain adequate protein and 
nutritional intake. 4. Monitor exercise performance. 
Health alert: 1. Evaluate muscle loss and physical performance. 2. Review the 
resistance training program. 3. Consider physiotherapy or a modified exercise 
plan. 5. Assess nutritional status and recovery. 
 
Body temperature:  
early intervention: 1. Adjust clothing and environmental comfort. 2. Maintain 
adequate hydration. 3. Avoid excessive physical exertion.  
Health alert: 1. Assess for infection-related symptoms. 2. Evaluate cabin 
temperature and environmental conditions. 3. Follow the appropriate 
infection-control or temperature management protocol. 
 
Radiation exposure: 
early intervention: 1. Identify the source of exposure. 2. Follow the scheduled 
dosimetry monitoring. 3. Review available shielding and exposure reduction 
measures. 
Health alert: 1. Assess the exposure level, duration, and radiation type. 2. 
Follow the emissions radiation emergency protocol. 3. Reduce further 
exposure using approved shading or relocation procedures. 4. Arrange 
medical evaluation based on the estimated dose. 
 
High tension stress:  
early intervention: 1. Take a short break from demanding tasks. 2. Practice 
controlled breathing or relaxation techniques. 3. Engage in a preferred 
recreational activity. 4. Maintain communication with crewmates. 
Health alert: 1. Identify possible causes such as workload, isolation , or sleep 
disruption. 2. Introduce stress management activities into the daily schedule. 
 
Sleep: 
early intervention: 1. Maintain a consistent sleep schedule. 2. Reduce screen 
exposure before sleeping. 3. Reduce unnecessary noise and light. 4. Avoid 
excessive caffeine intake. 5. Allow sufficient time for rest. 
Health alert: 1. Review the sleep schedule and workload. 2. Evaluate 
environmental factors affecting sleep. 3. Consider sleep monitoring and 
assessment. 
 
Social connection(loneliness): 
Early intervention: 1. Schedule social activities with crewmates. 2. Participate 
in Group recreational activities. 3. Maintain communication with family and 
friends on Earth when possible. 
 
Energy (drained): 
Early intervention: 1. Take a suitable rest period. 2. Maintain adequate 
hydration. 3. Eat balanced meals. 4. Adjust physical activity according to 
recovery needs. 5. Maintain a consistent sleep schedule. 
Health alert: 1. Evaluate sleep quality and workload. 2. Assess nutritional 
intake and hydration. 3. Investigate possible causes such as anemia or 
infection. 
 
* An important note: the website should never independently 
prescribe medication, change cabin oxygen levels, or modify 
radiation shielding procedures. Those actions must follow approved 
mission protocols and medical supervision.   
*A note for the astronaut: the abnormality percentage shouldn't 
delay emergency care, as serious symptoms or critical 
measurements must trigger immediate escalation even if the weekly 
abnormality percentage is low.
